The put and get method for a HashMap has an average of O(1) time complexity due to the fact that we hope each bucket stores a (key, value) pair according to the hash of the key. Accessing it in a set is thus O(1) since we already know the index of the (k,v) pair. The worst case is O(n) time complexity since in the case that all the key's hash fall under the same bucket. In this case, we have to loop through each pair inside of the bucket, making it linear complexity. In this scenario, we also have a rehash, which allows us to store more (key,value) pairs in case we go past the 80% capacity of the current hashmap. This allows us to keep the time complexity of put and get minimal by keeping less pairs in each bucket.
Merge sort has a time complexity of O(nlogn) for all cases of lists. Regardless of whether our lists are not sorted, nearly sorted, or fully sorted, our time complexity remains the same. Quick sort on the other hand has a best and average time complexity of O(nlogn), but has a worst case of O(n^2). Unlike merge sort, if the pivot of the quick sort is selected with a consistent pattern of choosing the last item of the partitioned sublist, the overall time complexity of a fully sorted list becomes O(n^2) since our partitioning only removes 1 digit every loop.
Merge sort can be preferred for guarantee of O(nlogn) regardless of the list case. Its independent of whether the list is sorted or not, giving O(nlogn). Its also stable in the case that there are duplicates in the list. On the other hand, quick sort uses less memory with in-place swapping, and the chances of worst case time complexity can be decreased through a random pivot.
